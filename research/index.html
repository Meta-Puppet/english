Preface

We started MetaPuppet as a VR experience. You bring a live camera image of yourself into VR and use your fingers to control the body you see. When we tried it, though, the phrase “controlling yourself” left something out.

What does it mean to control yourself here? The phrase started as a metaphor, but the experience gave us a reason to look at it more closely.

I move my fingers, and the motor and cable supply the force that lifts my forearm. The same person provides the input and receives the output. How does it feel to operate and be moved within the same body?

This page describes the apparatus I built to study that question and what we observed in a small exploratory pilot.

Chapter 01Roles

Two Roles in the Same Body

Body ownership, sense of agency, and self-location capture different aspects of how we experience our bodies. In MetaPuppet, we consider them separately.

You can feel that you initiated a movement with your fingers even as a motor moves your forearm. How strongly you identify the body you see as your own can also vary independently of how strongly you feel that you are moving it.

For this study, we use “acting self” for the person in the role of operating the device and “moved self” for the same person in the role of being moved by it. These working terms describe two roles that the apparatus brings together.

Chapter 02Design

Varying Viewpoint and Movement Independently

Participants wore a Meta Quest headset and saw their bodies in VR. We used two viewpoints: a first-person perspective (1PP) that followed the direction of the head and a third-person perspective (3PP) from a camera fixed behind the participant.

We also used three movement conditions.

Movement conditions
ConditionDescription
VoluntaryParticipants move their own forearms without the motor.
MediatedThe motor moves the forearm in response to finger input.
ReplayThe motor moves the forearm along a prerecorded command trajectory, independently of current finger input.

In the Mediated condition, Unity hand tracking measured the vertical movement of the pinching hand. The PC sent commands to the Raspberry Pi at 90 Hz. The Pi drove a motorized spool that pulled the forearm via a detachable harness. We recorded the hand input and command trajectories. Actual forearm displacement was unmeasured.

Chapter 03Pilot observation

Preliminary Observations

Six participants completed a seven-point rating scale after each condition. We asked about sense of agency, body ownership, feeling located in the visible body, feeling moved by an external force, feeling both in control and moved at the same time, and seeing one’s own body from the outside.

Mediated condition — mean ratings (1PP / 3PP)
Rating item1PP / 3PP
Sense of agency4.89 / 5.72
Sense of body ownership6.17 / 4.92
Feeling located in the visible body6.00 / 4.17
Feeling moved by an external force5.33 / 5.00

In the Mediated condition, mean sense of agency was higher in 3PP. Mean body ownership and the feeling of being located in the visible body were both lower in 3PP. The feeling of being moved by an external force remained high in both viewpoints.

Seeing the body from outside may have made it easier to follow the relationship between hand input and arm motion. The tactile and proprioceptive cues of being moved by the mechanism were still present. The differing patterns in agency and embodiment are relevant to the question this device was designed to investigate.

These are descriptive observations from the pilot. We ran no statistical tests, and the observations alone cannot establish that viewpoint separated the acting self from the moved self.

Chapter 04Scope

What the Pilot Leaves Open

The pilot had six participants, and everyone completed the conditions in a fixed order. We therefore could not distinguish effects of viewpoint from effects of familiarity or order. Our items drew on existing embodiment scales and included questions written for this apparatus. They have yet to be validated as a device-specific scale.

The ratings help identify what to vary and measure in the next study.

EpilogueOngoing work

Afterword

MetaPuppet lets the same person operate the device and be moved by it at the same time.

The experience depends on video, hand tracking, communication, motor control, and emergency stopping working together. A working apparatus makes it possible to turn impressions from the experience into questions we can test.

This pilot is a starting point. An apparatus that moves a person’s body in response to a movement they initiate gives us a concrete way to study sense of agency and body ownership separately.

We are now adapting the apparatus to use electromyography (EMG) and electrical muscle stimulation (EMS), with input from one part of the body used to move another. We want to examine whether people experience the initiating movement as a command to move the other body part or continue to experience it in its original role.
