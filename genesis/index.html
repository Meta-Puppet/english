Prologue 2026-04-02

At the Whiteboard

The mechanism first appeared as a red marker line rather than a metal appearance. On the whiteboard, next to the notes “perspective of control” and “integrity,” words such as agency, ownership, body-schema, and haptics are lined up, and a diagram connecting them with lines remains. At this point, the idea that would become the core of MetaPuppet, that “the self who moves” and the “self who is moved” coexist within the same body, was still nothing more than a relationship between words circled.

April 2, 2026, first whiteboard. The words agency, ownership, and body-schema are already listed.

This record uses only photographs to trace how the graffiti became the mechanism that actually moved a person's arm. I will leave the technical details (communication protocols and PD control details) to other articles, and here I will focus only on how the appearance of the mechanism itself has changed.

Chapter 01 Camera, first pass

Starting with the Camera

The first thing I moved my hand at was a camera, not a mechanism that moved my arm. The first prototype of a stereo camera that simply consists of two camera modules for Raspberry Pi placed side by side on a tripod head. There is no dedicated board or case yet, and the ribbon cable is left exposed.

First stereo camera prototype. A state where only two camera modules are lined up on a tripod head.

After that, I checked on my desk that it was connected directly to the Raspberry Pi.

Directly connected to the Raspberry Pi body
Chapter 02 Pole, first pass

Prototyping the Pole

In parallel with the camera, prototype production will begin on the mechanism that lifts the arm. It started with a very simple prototype, consisting of two green 3D-printed parts that bridged two poles and a round cap attached to the end.

First prototype of the pole-type mechanism. Checking the range of motion by holding the tip cap in hand.
Chapter 03 Standing the frame

Assembling the Frame

The prototype, which was tested with the pole lying down, eventually stood up from the floor. This photo shows two poles set vertically using a shelf and connected at the top with a crossbar. Two green 3D-printed mounts are attached to the crossbar, with yellow strings hanging there for testing, but no servos are mounted yet.

The state in which the pole was erected for the first time. The crossbar mount doesn't have a servo yet, and I'm checking the range of motion by just passing the string through.
Chapter 04 Spool, before the servo

Testing the Spool Before Adding the Servo

Before incorporating the servo, we first tested the thread winding mechanism itself using a different set of poles. Inside the green mount, you can see a small shaft with thread wrapped around it. At this point, the motor is not connected yet.

A prototype with only the thread winding mechanism removed. motor is not connected yet
Chapter 05 The servo and the drum

Integrating the Servo and Drum

In parallel with the verification of the thread winding, the actual servos and drums that will be used are being matched. The servo is Feetech's STS3215 (7.4V, gear ratio 1:191). This photo shows a blue 3D-printed drum temporarily assembled on the output shaft, with spiral grooves cut into the side of the drum to align the cables, and holes lined up on the end for wiring and shafts to pass through.

The first blue 3D printed drum I assembled with the STS3215 servo. Align cables with side spiral grooves
Chapter 06 Updated mount

Redesigning the Mount

Once the servo and drum were assembled, the mount on the frame side was also updated. The Raspberry Pi now has a cooling case with a heatsink and fan attached to a blue mount. The green mount on the crossbar side has a hole inside to pass the yellow cable through.

Raspberry Pi with cooling case and updated motor mount
Chapter 07 Old vs. new mount

Comparing the Green and Gray Mounts

The first green mount (left) had a place for the servo, but the drum was also small. The remade gray mount (right) has a shape that allows the servo to fit securely, and a structure that supports the shaft on the opposite side with a ball bearing has been added. Comparing the two drums in the foreground, the white drum on the right is slightly larger.

Left: First green mount (small drum). Right: Rebuilt gray mount (servo mounting, ball bearings, larger drum)
Chapter 08 Four channels

Expanding from Two Channels to Four

All the prototypes so far were based on two systems of servos (ID 5 and 6). The current mechanism has been expanded to four systems: A, B, C, and D. Each of the four poles has an independent mount and can be distinguished by color-coded letters.

Current 4 system configuration. A, B, C, and D mounts are lined up.
